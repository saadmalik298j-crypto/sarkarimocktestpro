3. Core High-Yield Exam Formulas & Worked Examples

Profit, Loss & Markup

Profit & Loss % P/CP × 100 | L/CP × 100
Selling Price (SP) CP × (100 ± P/L)%
Markup & Discount SP = MP × (1 − D/100)
Successive Discounts (d1, d2) D = d1 + d2 − (d1×d2)/100
CP to MP Relation CP/MP = (100 − D)/(100 + P)
Dishonest Dealer Gain % [Error / (True − Error)] × 100
Worked Example (CP/MP Shortcut): An article is sold at a 10% discount and yields a 20% profit. Find the markup % over Cost Price.
Formula: CP/MP = (100 − 10)/(100 + 20) = 90/120 = 3/4.
If CP = 3 units, MP = 4 units. Markup = (4 − 3)/3 = 1/3 = 33.33%.

Ratio, Proportion & Alligation

Mean Proportional b = √(a × c)
Third Proportional c = b² / a
Fourth Proportional d = (b × c) / a
Compounded Ratio (a/b) × (c/d) = ac/bd
Rule of Alligation (Cheaper Q) / (Dearer Q) = (d − m) / (m − c)
Repeated Dilution Remaining = Initial × (1 − x/V)ⁿ
Worked Example (Alligation): Tea at $60/kg is mixed with tea at $85/kg to produce a mixture worth $75/kg. Find the mixing ratio.
Ratio: (Cheaper) : (Dearer) = (85 − 75) : (75 − 60) = 10 : 15 = 2 : 3.

Speed, Time & Distance

Unit Conversion 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s | 1 m/s = 18/5 km/h
Average Speed (Equal Distances) 2xy / (x + y)
Relative Speed (Opposite) S₁ + S₂ (approaching)
Relative Speed (Same Direction) |S₁ − S₂| (overtaking)
Train Crossing Platform (Lp) Time = (Lₕ + Lᵖ) / Speed
Boats: Downstream & Upstream D = B + S | U = B − S | B = (D+U)/2
Worked Example (Average Speed): A cyclist goes from A to B at 20 km/h and returns at 30 km/h. Find average speed for the round trip.
Shortcut: 2xy / (x + y) = (2 × 20 × 30) / (20 + 30) = 1200 / 50 = 24 km/h (NOT 25 km/h!).

4. Five Essential Speed-Math Mental Calculation Tricks

Mastering these mental arithmetic techniques enables aspirants to bypass pen-and-paper scratchwork and gain 5 to 10 seconds on each question.

Trick 1: Squaring Any Number Ending in 5

Rule: To square a two-digit or multi-digit integer represented as N5, multiply the leading digits N by (N + 1) and append 25 to the end.

Original Example: Compute 75² and 115²
• For 75: N = 7. Compute 7 × (7 + 1) = 7 × 8 = 56. Append 25 → 5,625.
• For 115: N = 11. Compute 11 × 12 = 132. Append 25 → 13,225.

Trick 2: Rapid Multiplication by 11 (The Sandwich Method)

Rule: Keep the first and last digits in place. Every intermediate digit is the sum of adjacent digit pairs from right to left, carrying forward any values ≥ 10.

Original Example: Multiply 4,352 by 11
1. Rightmost digit is 2.
2. 5 + 2 = 7.
3. 3 + 5 = 8.
4. 4 + 3 = 7.
5. Leftmost digit is 4.
Combine: 47,872. (No paper scratchwork needed!)

Trick 3: Multiplication of Numbers Near Base 100

Rule: For numbers (100 + a) and (100 + b): the left part is 100 + a + b, and the right two digits are a × b.

Original Example: Multiply 107 × 108
• Base deviations: +7 and +8.
• Left side: 107 + 8 = 115.
• Right side: 7 × 8 = 56.
Concatenate: 11,556.
Sub-100 variation: 96 × 94 (deviations −4 and −6). Left: 96 − 6 = 90. Right: (−4) × (−6) = 24. Result = 9,024.

Trick 4: Units Sum to 10 & Tens Digits Identical

Rule: For products of two-digit numbers where tens digits are equal (a) and unit digits satisfy b + c = 10: Product = [a × (a + 1)] × 100 + (b × c).

Original Example: Calculate 64 × 66
• Tens digits = 6 (identical); Units = 4 + 6 = 10.
• Left block: 6 × (6 + 1) = 42.
• Right block: 4 × 6 = 24.
Combine: 4,224.

Trick 5: The Percentage Interchange & Fractional Split Method

Rule: Because percentage multiplication is commutative, X% of Y = Y% of X. When calculating an awkward percentage of a friendly number, reverse the operation or split into base building blocks (50%, 10%, 5%, 1%).

Original Example: Find 68% of 25 and 43% of 450
• Part A: 68% of 25 = 25% of 68 = 1/4 of 68 = 17.
• Part B: 43% of 450 = Split 43% into (50% − 5% − 2%).
    50% of 450 = 225.0
    5% of 450 = 22.5
    2% of 450 = 9.0 (since 1% = 4.5)
    Total = 225 − 22.5 − 9 = 202.5 − 9 = 193.5.

5. Five Fatal Calculation Mistakes in SSC & Banking (And How to Avoid Them)

Negative marking in SSC and sectional timers in Banking penalize recurring conceptual traps. Review these five classic pitfalls:

Trap 1: The Asymmetric Percentage Base Shift Error

Common Mistake: Assuming that if Price of Sugar increases by 25%, consumption must reduce by 25% to maintain constant expenditure.

Correct Method: When the base increases by fraction a/b, the offsetting reduction to restore the original value is a / (a + b).
Proof: 25% increase = +1/4. Offsetting reduction = 1 / (4 + 1) = 1/5 = 20%. (Never 25%).
Trap 2: Using Arithmetic Mean for Average Speed Over Equal Distances

Common Mistake: Averaging speeds directly as (v1 + v2) / 2 when covering the same distance at different velocities.

Correct Method: Average speed is Total Distance ÷ Total Time. When distances are equal, the average is the harmonic mean: 2 × v1 × v2 / (v1 + v2).
Example: Driving to office at 60 km/h and returning at 40 km/h is NOT 50 km/h; it is 48 km/h: (2 × 60 × 40) / 100 = 4800 / 100 = 48.
Trap 3: Adding Successive Discounts Directly

Common Mistake: Assuming two consecutive discounts of 20% and 10% equate to a single discount of 30%.

Correct Method: Successive discounts compound on the diminishing balance: Net D% = d1 + d2 − (d1 × d2)/100.
Example: 20 + 10 − (20 × 10)/100 = 30 − 2 = 28%. Direct addition inflates the discount by 2% and ruins profit/loss answers.
Trap 4: Calculating Dishonest Dealer Profit on True Weight Instead of Given Weight

Common Mistake: If a grocer promises 1000g but dispenses 800g, calculating profit % as (200 / 1000) × 100 = 20%.

Correct Method: The seller's actual cost basis is only the weight delivered (800g). Profit % = [Error / True Value Sold] × 100 = [200 / 800] × 100 = 25%.
Always divide the discrepancy by what leaves the shop, not what the buyer requested.
Trap 5: Neglecting Train Length When Crossing Platforms and Bridges

Common Mistake: Taking distance as only the length of the platform when computing the time taken by a train to cross it.

Correct Method: A point object (pole, standing person) requires traversing only train length Lt. A platform or tunnel requires traversing Lt + Lp.
Example: A 200m train travelling at 54 km/h (15 m/s) crossing a 300m bridge takes (200 + 300) / 15 = 500 / 15 = 33.33 seconds. Using just 300m yields 20s, which is an immediate exam distractor choice.
